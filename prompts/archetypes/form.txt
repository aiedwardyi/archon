REQUIRED FLOW STRUCTURE
═══════════════════════════════════════

1. SPLIT WIZARD LAYOUT
- Left rail for progress and reassurance.
- Right main panel for the active step and form content.
- The UI should feel like a premium onboarding or setup flow, not a plain contact form.
- For enterprise onboarding, approval, or compliance flows, the right side should usually behave like a 2-part work area: dominant active-step pane plus a compact summary/snapshot/status lane that stays visible on desktop.
- The right main panel must be the dominant focal area and occupy roughly 65-75% of the visible width on desktop.
- NEVER center one narrow floating card in a sea of empty dark space.
- Reuse the provided split-layout classes directly: `.wizard-shell`, `.wizard-frame`, `.wizard-side`, `.wizard-main`.
- Do not set `max-width` on `.wizard-shell`, `.wizard-frame`, `.wizard-main`, `.main-wrapper`, or equivalent top-level containers below about `1180px` on desktop.
- Do not replace the split shell with a single-column wrapper unless the viewport is mobile.
- Do not shrink or trivialize the top bar. The desktop screenshot should show a substantial product header with readable branding and an obvious compact progress treatment.
- Favor an enterprise product shell with a compact top bar, project/application meta, and persistent desktop structure over a startup-marketing hero.

2. STEP PROGRESSION
- Show 4-5 steps with one active, prior steps complete, later steps pending.
- Include progress percentage or progress meter.
- Use meaningful step labels, not Step 1 / Step 2 only.
- Avoid generic step titles like `Step 1`, `Details`, `Review`, and `Done` with no domain context. The labels should reflect the actual setup flow.
- The active step must feel like a real product setup checkpoint, not a generic card in the middle of the page.

3. ACTIVE FORM STEP
- At least 4 form controls on the main step.
- Mix control types: text input, segmented choice, card selection, toggle, textarea, checklist, or integration tile.
- One field must show a validation/help/error state.
- Include clear primary and secondary actions.
- The main panel must include a strong section heading, a short explanatory subheading, and visibly grouped subsections so the page does not read as one long flat slab.
- Prefer one large primary pane with nested cards/rows over many unrelated mini cards.
- Use exactly this internal rhythm when possible:
  top summary row -> primary configuration section -> integrations/settings section -> validation/result section.
- At least one subsection must use a 2-column internal layout with clearly aligned labels and controls.
- Do not render every stage as a fully expanded long page. Keep the active flow compact enough that the user can understand the setup state without scrolling through a giant vertical stack.
- At most 2 dense content sections should be fully expanded in the main pane at once; later-state material should be previewed as a compact status panel, confirmation tray, or collapsed review block.

4. SUPPORTING CONTEXT
- Left side should include benefits, checklist, or trust/progress messaging.
- Add a summary or signal card showing the current configuration in non-zero terms.
- Include one premium state treatment such as validation log lines, success notice, or readiness score.
- Keep one compact review, blocker, readiness, or next-step card visible before the final submit state so the current setup status is obvious at a glance.
- For onboarding, approvals, or compliance flows, include a visible application snapshot, blocker summary, or requirements/status rail on desktop instead of hiding all context below the fold.
- Side rail content must be visually substantial: progress rail, current plan, readiness summary, and reassurance copy. Avoid empty dark columns.

5. SUCCESS / RESULT VISIBILITY
- Even if the main step is mid-flow, include a visible preview of the success/result state:
  confirmation card, launch summary, next-step panel, or generated workspace snapshot.
- The success/result preview must feel integrated into the same product flow, not pasted underneath as a disconnected card.
- Do not render validating, success, and failure as three separate full-height sections stacked one after another. Show them as compact preview/status cards inside the same flow.

═══════════════════════════════════════
COMPLIANCE / APPROVAL VARIANT
═══════════════════════════════════════

Apply this variant when the prompt mentions vendor onboarding, compliance documents, approval routing, blocker summary, application snapshot, requirements summary, or review-and-submit gating.

- Keep a compact top product bar with application or workflow identity visible on desktop.
- Use domain-specific step names such as company details, compliance documents, contacts and approvals, review and submit.
- Keep a persistent desktop snapshot or status lane that shows meaningful rows like company flags, document counts, pending requirements, approvals, blockers, or readiness state.
- If the prompt mentions documents or approvals, surface pending counts and approval state with badges, counts, and status rows, not just helper paragraphs.
- Keep unresolved blockers visible by default. The user should be able to see why submission is blocked without navigating to a separate screen.
- The active step should still dominate, but the status lane must stay visually substantial enough to read at a glance in a full-page screenshot.
- Avoid startup-marketing onboarding language when the flow is really about requirements, approvals, and gated submission.

═══════════════════════════════════════
INTERACTION RULES
═══════════════════════════════════════

- Buttons, chips, and selectable cards must have hover/focus/active states.
- The active step must be visually obvious.
- Show at least one dynamic-feeling state: validating, syncing, ready, or confirmed.
- Inputs must have clear focus styling and helper/error text.
- Card surfaces must show clear elevation differences: outer shell, active step panel, nested settings groups, and success/result preview should not all share the same flat treatment.
- Use one disciplined accent system only. Prefer deep navy surfaces with electric blue or green signals, not many unrelated accent colors.
- Pick ONE primary accent family for the whole flow and stick to it. Do not use green selected cards with blue buttons or mixed blue/green CTAs in the same desktop frame.
- Use larger section headings with clear hierarchy. Small utility typography alone is not enough for this archetype.
- The main page heading and each section heading must be visually substantial in the screenshot. Avoid tiny card-label typography carrying the whole flow.
- Make the page heading clearly larger than section headings, and section headings clearly larger than labels/helper text. The screenshot should read like a designed product flow from a distance, not a wall of small UI text.
- Validation, success, and failure states must feel intentionally designed, with stronger containers and spacing than regular form rows.
- Give the active panel and at least one nested card a richer surface treatment: layered shadow, soft inner highlight, and subtle tonal gradient so the flow feels productized rather than flat.
- The page should read as a guided setup sequence, not a documentation page. If the layout starts becoming a long article of controls, reduce the number of open subsections and increase hierarchy.
- Avoid tiny brand labels, tiny step labels, or undersized summary text in a large desktop shell. The layout should still feel substantial from a distant screenshot.
- Keep supporting summaries compact. Do not let review, validation, success, and failure all expand into equally tall blocks with the same visual weight.
- Final state sections should feel like polished status cards, not tiny text islands floating in empty space.
- Avoid giant empty vertical gaps inside the main panel. If a section is short, the next relevant section should follow with deliberate spacing instead of leaving large dark voids.
- Keep the active step, review block, and result preview visually connected in one cohesive column. Do not separate them with screen-sized whitespace.
- Do not expand validation, review, success, and failure into four equally tall panels. Later-state surfaces should stay compact and subordinate to the active step.
- Prefer a product-like review sidebar or snapshot panel over a generic “summary card” if the prompt implies approvals, documents, blockers, or submission gating.

═══════════════════════════════════════
CONTENT RULES
═══════════════════════════════════════

- ZERO-VALUE BAN: no 0 connected apps, 0 steps complete, or empty summaries.
- Use realistic setup data: team size, workspace URL, plan names, integration counts, launch dates.
- Avoid generic hero marketing copy. This is a flow/product screen first.
- Keep the page grounded in function, but polished enough to feel premium.
- NEVER let labels, helper text, summary rows, or state messages overlap, truncate awkwardly, or clip. Broken spacing immediately fails this archetype.
- Prefer premium onboarding product language like workspace, launch, environment, integrations, validation, ready state, next steps.
- Avoid cramped stacked checkboxes, dense bullet lists, or tiny helper text blocks. If the content feels crowded, simplify and enlarge.
