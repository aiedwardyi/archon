REQUIRED WORKSPACE STRUCTURE
═══════════════════════════════════════

1. TOPBAR
- Product/editor name at left.
- Save state, publish button, and at least 2 utility actions at right.
- Include one visible "last saved" status and one collaborator/presence cue.
- Topbar must feel structurally integrated with the workspace below. Avoid a floating disconnected control strip.
- Topbar should share the same alignment grid as the three columns beneath it.

2. THREE-PANEL LAYOUT
- Left sidebar for outline, views, or document navigation.
- Center editor canvas as the main focal area.
- Right inspector/comments panel for metadata, comments, or publishing settings.
- The center panel must dominate visually.
- Column widths and vertical alignment must feel intentional. Avoid mismatched top edges or panels that look pasted beside the canvas.
- On desktop, the left sidebar and right inspector must both be persistently visible in the screenshot. Do not hide them behind drawers, overlay panels, toggles, or off-canvas interactions as the default state.
- Do not leave either side panel mostly empty. The user should immediately see useful populated outline rows on the left and useful comments/settings cards on the right.
- Reuse `.workspace-grid`, `.sidebar`, `.editor-main`, and `.inspector` as the default desktop structure.
- Do not turn outline/comments into modals, overlays, popovers, or hidden panels for the primary screenshot.
- Do not constrain `.editor-main` to a single narrow centered column by removing the visible side panels.

3. TOOLBAR
- Formatting or content controls with active states.
- At least 6 controls such as heading, bold, highlight, slash command, comment, export.
- One active control must be visibly selected.
- Toolbar controls should feel grouped and premium, not like default pill buttons repeated in a row.
- Do not center a generic floating toolbar as the main identity move. The topbar should feel productized and integrated, with stronger brand/title treatment and more distinctive action group styling.
- Keep the topbar concise. Fewer, better-grouped actions beat a long row of similar circular buttons.

4. DOCUMENT CANVAS
- Large editorial-style title, subtitle/meta row, and multiple content blocks.
- Use real-looking structured content, not lorem ipsum.
- Include mixed block types: paragraph, checklist, quote, metric card, inline callout, or embed preview.
- Show a clear editing affordance such as a selected block, insertion marker, floating action, or comment anchor.
- No broken copy, clipped metadata, malformed inline HTML, or accidental raw markup. Text integrity is mandatory.
- Use one strong accent color system with restrained highlights for comments, active tools, and publish status.
- Document title must feel dramatic and editorial. Do not undersize it.
- Include at least one clearly visible inline editor affordance near content: comment marker, slash trigger, selected block edge, inline chip, or floating insertion control.
- The document should not read like a static article screenshot. Make at least two active editing states obvious at a glance: selected text/tooling, comment target, insertion handle, slash prompt, or publish/save feedback.
- Do not solve this archetype with a mostly white or beige document page plus a few colored buttons. The chrome and canvas need deliberate contrast so the workspace feels like a premium product, not a print preview.
- Avoid fake decorative collaborator cursors, random colored bars, or novelty markers that do not improve the editing workflow. Prefer real product cues like active comments, selection highlights, status chips, slash-menu hints, or review markers.
- Inline collaboration markers should be subtle and product-like. Do not place loud standalone name tags such as "Alice" or "Bob" floating inside the document body.

5. SUPPORTING PANELS
- Left outline with 4+ sections and one active row.
- Right panel with at least 2 cards, such as comments, SEO/publish settings, collaborators, or revision notes.
- Include timestamps, avatars/initials, and non-zero counts.
